Email Spoofing

🎣 Phase 1 — The Bait

You just received this email. Read it carefully. Does anything seem suspicious? Try to spot the red flags before proceeding.

mail.example-corp.com/inbox
security-alert@paypa1-secure.com
you@yourcompany.com
Mon, 18 Aug 2026, 02:47 AM
support@paypa1-recover.net
⚠️ Urgent: Your PayPal account has been temporarily suspended

Dear Valued Customer,

We have detected unusual login activity on your PayPal account from an unrecognised device in Lagos, Nigeria. As a precautionary measure, we have temporarily suspended access to your account.

To restore full access, you must verify your identity within 24 hours — failure to do so will result in permanent account closure and forfeiture of any remaining balance.

Verify My Account Now

This link will expire in 24 hours. If you did not initiate this request, please contact us immediately at support@paypa1-secure.com

PayPal Holdings Inc. | 2211 North First Street | San Jose, CA 95131

© 2026 PayPal. All rights reserved.

🔍 Phase 2 — The Reveal

This email is a fake. Here are the 6 red flags that expose it as a phishing attempt.

🚩
1. Spoofed Sender Domain
The from address is security-alert@paypa1-secure.com — note the number "1" instead of the letter "l" in "paypal". This is typosquatting. Real PayPal only sends from @paypal.com.
🚩
2. Suspicious Reply-To Address
The Reply-To is a completely different domain: support@paypa1-recover.net. Legitimate organisations never redirect replies to different domains.
🚩
3. Urgency & Fear Tactics
"24 hours or permanent closure", "forfeiture of remaining balance" — these are classic fear induction tactics designed to make you act without thinking. Legitimate services give you time and multiple ways to verify.
🚩
4. Vague Greeting
"Dear Valued Customer" — PayPal always addresses you by your full name. Generic greetings are a dead giveaway that the attacker doesn't actually have your personal info.
🚩
5. The "Verify" Button Goes Nowhere Safe
The blue "Verify My Account Now" button links to a credential harvesting page — not paypal.com. Always hover over buttons to inspect the destination URL before clicking.
🚩
6. Suspicious In-Email Contact Link
The support link at the bottom points to the same fake domain. This is a trap — even the "help" links funnel you to the attacker's infrastructure.

🛡️ Phase 3 — How to Defend Against Email Spoofing

Apply these habits and technical measures to protect yourself and your organisation.

  • Always inspect the full sender domain — not just the display name. Attackers can set any display name.
  • Hover over all links before clicking. The destination URL must match the legitimate domain exactly.
  • Go directly to the service's website (type it in your browser) instead of clicking email links.
  • Enable email authentication: SPF, DKIM, and DMARC on your domain to prevent spoofing outbound.
  • Use a spam filter that checks for SPF/DKIM alignment — many phishing emails fail these checks.
  • Never act on urgency framed in email. Call the company directly using the number on their official website.
  • Report suspicious emails using your email client's "Report Phishing" button.
